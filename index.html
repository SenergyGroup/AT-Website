About Advancing Together

A collaborative built to move from insight to implementation.

Advancing Together brings together representatives from the business, nonprofit, public, health, and community sectors to address the conditions that shape well-being across the Cedar Valley. The coalition is rooted in the belief that healthy people require a healthy community and that inequity is created by systems that can be changed.

The current structure reflects the 2025 combination of Advancing Equity and the Community Health Improvement steering committee. That merger created a more unified model for coordinating partners, connecting residents to resources, and focusing community energy around shared outcomes.

Current CHIP Priorities

Three priority areas, one shared commitment to long-term well-being.

Each priority includes goals, measurable objectives, and strategies that span systems change, community partnerships, organizational coordination, and individual support.

01

2026-2028 CHIP

Economic Security

Advance financial stability by strengthening connections to work, benefits, supports, and resource navigation that help residents move toward greater security.

  • Reduce barriers that make it hard to maintain income and essential supports at the same time.
  • Coordinate services so residents are not left navigating disconnected systems on their own.
  • Build strategies that respond to real community conditions, not assumptions about need.
02

2026-2028 CHIP

Housing Stability

Support stable, healthy housing by addressing the factors that influence safety, affordability, neighborhood conditions, and access to responsive services.

  • Surface the structural drivers that keep some neighborhoods under-resourced and disconnected.
  • Improve coordination across housing, health, and support systems.
  • Center resident experience when defining what stability and safety look like in practice.
03

2026-2028 CHIP

Transportation Challenges

Improve mobility so people can reliably reach employment, healthcare, education, childcare, and daily essentials without transportation barriers driving instability.

  • Identify gaps in routes, access, and navigation that limit opportunity across the region.
  • Connect transportation planning to health, housing, and economic outcomes.
  • Support practical solutions that improve day-to-day access for residents and frontline partners.

How the Roadmap Was Built

The CHIP roadmap was shaped through a collaborative, data-driven process.

The core team worked with Advancing Together participants to deepen understanding of each priority through local data, community feedback, and shared learning activities such as the A Walk in Their Shoes poverty simulation.

1

Listen to residents and navigators

Issue profiles were informed by the experiences of residents and frontline partners closest to the work.

2

Review local data and common challenges

Partners identified shared patterns across the priority areas and used that insight to focus strategy.

3

Align with work already underway

Existing community initiatives were connected to the plan to reduce duplication and increase impact.

4

Track progress transparently

Ongoing data review, community input, and public reporting help keep the plan practical and responsive.

Our Story

From Advancing Equity to Advancing Together.

The current roadmap builds on several years of systems mapping, community reflection, and coalition development across the Cedar Valley.

2018

A regional wake-up call

The community response accelerated after Waterloo-Cedar Falls was named the worst place for Black Americans to live.

2019-2023

Systems mapping and strategic action

Local stories and data were used to identify patterns, bright spots, and theories for change through the Advancing Equity process.

2025

One merged direction

Advancing Equity and the Community Health Improvement steering committee combined efforts to create a more coordinated structure.

2026-2028

Implementation through the CHIP roadmap

Advancing Together now carries the work forward through clear priorities, measurable objectives, and aligned community action.

What the earlier work taught us

Healthy communities require systems that are fair, connected, and responsive.

The original Advancing Equity work helped the community name the patterns driving inequitable outcomes and the strengths that can support change. Those lessons continue to shape the current roadmap.

Divided We Fall

Segregation and disconnection continue to shape opportunity, belonging, and trust.

Holes in the Safety Net

Communities pay the price when healthcare, housing, education, and income stability are uneven.

Impact of Bias

Bias influences access, treatment, safety, and long-term health in visible and invisible ways.

Seeds of Resilience

When people and organizations align around need, new resources and practical solutions become possible.
