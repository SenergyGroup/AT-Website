Where the CHIP priorities live.

The 2026-2028 CHIP work is organized around three community priorities. Each priority has a lead table, and Advancing Together also hosts action-planning groups that move specific Economic Stability strategies forward.

Economic Stability: Advancing Together Transportation: TAC Housing: Mayor's Homelessness Coalition
§ 01 CHIP Priority Homes

Three priorities, three lead tables, and one shared community health improvement process.

01

Economic Stability

Advancing Together leads the Economic Stability priority, with action-planning groups focused on the systems, partnerships, resources, and community leadership needed to reduce economic barriers.

  • Coordinates Health Sector, Funding, Workforce, Communications, Lived Experiences, and Policy action-planning lanes.
  • Connects partner work around resource navigation, workforce access, prevention, public materials, funding, and lived experience leadership.
  • Uses action plans and public updates to keep implementation visible over the Q1-Q10 CHIP timeline.
Lead: Advancing Together
02

Housing Stability

Housing Stability stays aligned through the Mayor's Homelessness Coalition, which provides the lead table for partners working on homelessness, housing access, and structural barriers to stable housing.

  • Supports coordination among housing, health, social service, public, and community partners.
  • Focuses on homelessness reduction, safe and stable housing access, and system barriers that make stability harder to reach.
  • Connects housing work back to the broader CHIP roadmap without duplicating the coalition's existing table.
Lead: Mayor's Homelessness Coalition
03

Transportation Challenges

Transportation work is led through the Transportation Advisory Committee, which keeps mobility planning connected to access to jobs, healthcare, education, childcare, and daily needs.

  • Aligns public transit, passenger transportation, rider feedback, service coordination, and community education efforts.
  • Identifies practical barriers that prevent residents from reliably reaching essential destinations.
  • Connects transportation planning to the health, housing, and economic outcomes named in the CHIP roadmap.
Lead: Transportation Advisory Committee
